# Mobbin Research: Marketing Website Patterns for Cactus Peak Athletic

Scope: web marketing patterns for a 5-club athletic club in Mesa and Gilbert, Arizona. Audience: East Valley adults 25-55, families, professionals, teens, older members.

## Method and limits

- Ran 23 Mobbin searches on platform `web`: 14 screen searches and 9 section searches.
- Looked at the returned preview images. Patterns below come only from what the previews showed.
- Mobbin returned few true gym or club websites. Most fitness hits were booking tools (Time2book, Open, Fresha, Sweatpals), ClassPass, and Care.com. Other results came from adjacent categories: retail store locators, restaurant finders, hotel search, coworking, pricing pages, and footers.
- Previews show only the top of each page. Full pages were not read. Check each pattern on the live source before we copy it.
- Mobile evidence is thin. Only one mobile-width screen came back (Hims drawer). Run a follow-up search before final mobile decisions.

## 2. Pattern catalog

### Navigation

**N1. Persistent header with one filled CTA.**
- Layout: logo left. Four or five text links center or right. One filled pill button at far right that stays in the same spot on every page.
- Copy style: short verbs and nouns ("Find classes", "Plans", "Log in", "Try for free").
- Sources: ClassPass (S1) on every captured page. Time2book (S3) uses the same idea with "Sign in" as a ghost button and "Start free trial" filled.
- Why a member values it: one clear way to start, on every page, without hunting.

**N2. Mega menu with columns and a promo card.**
- Layout: full-width dropdown under the top bar. Three or four columns of links with small headings. One right-side card with a photo, a short headline, and a single link.
- Sources: Hers (S22) uses four columns plus a "Take the free hair quiz" card. lululemon (S22) uses large links left, grouped headings middle, and a product card right.
- Why a member values it: the menu shows the club's full range (classes, clubs, programs) without opening a new page.

**N3. Search dropdown with icon rows.**
- Layout: search field at top. Dropdown lists categories, each with a small icon, bold title, and one-line subtitle.
- Sources: komoot (S22) lists Hiking, Cycling, Running with icons and subtitles.
- Why a member values it: a member can pick an activity type ("Cycling", "Swim") and see what it means.

**N4. Mobile drawer with horizontal card rows.**
- Layout: full-height drawer. Header row with account and cart icons and a close X. Section titles, then horizontal scroll rows of cards.
- Sources: Hims (S23). Only one mobile-width capture. Treat as weak evidence.
- Why a member values it: quick access to the main sections on a phone.

**N5. Top promo bar above the header.**
- Layout: thin colored bar with one line of text and one small button. Sits above the logo bar.
- Sources: Hers (S22) shows a lime bar with a "Get started" button. Hims (S23) shows a bar of trust points.
- Why a member values it: announces a trial, a new club, or a holiday schedule change.

### Hero

**H1. Headline, one support line, two buttons.**
- Layout: big headline left or centered. One line of support text under it. Filled primary button and outline secondary button side by side.
- Copy style: "Explore experiences" and "Host an experience" (Sweatpals, S4). "Start free trial" and "Book a demo" (Time2book, S3).
- Why a member values it: the first action is obvious. A second option serves members who want to look first.

**H2. Headline with a row of three checkmarked trust points.**
- Layout: centered headline. One row of three check icons with short proof points under it. Two split cards below, each with a single CTA.
- Sources: Headspace (S16) uses this layout with "1,000+ meditations", "Affordable therapy that accepts insurance", and "Trusted by 105M+ downloads".
- Why a member values it: proof appears before the first click.

**H3. Featured program card as hero.**
- Layout: wide card. Photo on the left half. Title, program line, and a short description on the right. One "See details" button.
- Sources: Open (S2) uses this for a featured program.
- Why a member values it: a clear entry point to one class series.

**H4. Quiz or self-check call to action in the hero.**
- Layout: large headline. One filled button labeled "Take the quiz". A small privacy note below the button.
- Sources: Hers (S22).
- Why a member values it: a low-effort first step. The note tells people what happens to their answers.

**H5. Full-bleed photo with large overlay type.**
- Layout: full-width photo, big headline over it.
- Sources: Posh (screen 27dd8802 in the hero search). Legibility drops where the headline crosses busy areas in the photo.
- Why a member values it: strong emotion from people in motion. Keep the headline clear of faces and busy detail.

### Locations

**L1. Split view: list on the left, map on the right, count bubbles on clusters.**
- Layout: list column on the left with cards. Map on the right. Clusters show a number bubble. Single pins show a small label or icon.
- Sources: H&M (S9) uses count bubbles and a list with a plus button. Walmart (S7) and sweetgreen (S8) use a list with a map. Care.com (S5) adds filters on the left.
- Why a member values it: the member can see where the clubs are and compare them in one view.

**L2. Location card with status line and distance.**
- Layout: bold club name. Street address in regular text. Status line with color and words. Distance in miles. Link or button for details.
- Status copy seen: "Open · until 11pm" in green with "1.73 miles away" (Walmart, S7). "Closed opens at 10.00am" in orange (Fresha, S6). "Open · Closes at 3:40 PM" (DoorDash, S12).
- Why a member values it: they know if the club is open right now, and how far it is, before opening the page.

**L3. Filter chips with icons.**
- Layout: row of rounded chips above the list. Each chip has an icon and a label. Chips toggle on and off.
- Sources: Walmart (S7) lists services as chips (Auto Care, Bakery, Deli, Pharmacy, Gas station, Vision Center, Photo Center). Urban Outfitters (S10) uses checkboxes with icons for store features and greys out unavailable ones.
- Why a member values it: a member can filter by what matters (pool, courts, cycling, kids club).

**L4. Location search with distance stepper.**
- Layout: left filter panel. Location field, then a distance control with minus and plus buttons (for example "10 miles").
- Sources: Care.com (S5).
- Why a member values it: quick control over how far to search, without typing.

**L5. Location photo cards in a grid.**
- Layout: three or four columns. Each card has a large photo, a city or place name, and optional small text (country, open roles, address).
- Sources: Wise (S13) shows a city link, a country flag, and a status dot with a count. Udemy (S13) shows a photo with a bold city name under it. Oyster (S13) overlays the name on the photo. Zendesk (S13) shows three photos with city labels below.
- Why a member values it: a fast visual scan. A member finds their city by its look.

**L6. Office or site grid with headquarters called out.**
- Layout: one large card for the main site, with smaller cards around it. Each card has a photo, a place name, and an address in small text.
- Sources: OpenTable (S14) puts the San Francisco headquarters in a large card.
- Why a member values it: the main club can stand out from the others.

**L7. Store hours table with today highlighted.**
- Layout: small table. Each row is a day with hours. The current day is bold.
- Sources: Apple (S11) shows "Today" first with bold hours. Monte (S24) shows weekday and weekend hours, plus holiday hours below.
- Why a member values it: a member can check whether a club is open on a given day.

**L8. Hover-based city list with address card.**
- Layout: large type list of city names. The hovered name is highlighted. A card below shows the address, phone, and email.
- Sources: Craft Agency (S13).
- Why a member values it: a quick address lookup. This works on desktop only. Do not use it as the only way to choose a club on mobile.

### Location detail

**D1. Tab bar at the top of a location page.**
- Layout: row of text tabs under the club name: for example Photos, Services, Team, Reviews, About (Fresha, S6). Or Overview, Reviews, Location (Care.com, S5).
- Why a member values it: each kind of information has its own place. The page stays short.

**D2. About block with map, address, and hours.**
- Layout: a short paragraph. A map with one pin. Address line with a "Get directions" link. Opening times list with one row per day. A short "Additional information" list with check icons.
- Sources: Fresha (S6) "About" tab. Items seen: "Instant Confirmation", "Pay by app".
- Why a member values it: the answers to "where", "when", and "how do I book" are on one screen.

**D3. Sticky booking card on the right.**
- Layout: card in the right column. Rating line at top. One primary black or filled button ("Book now"). Status line. Address with "Get directions".
- Sources: Fresha (S6). Care.com (S5) uses a date picker card with a Series or Drop-ins toggle and a price per child.
- Why a member values it: the main action stays visible while the member reads.

**D4. Services list with duration, price, and Book button.**
- Layout: category pills across the top (for example Featured, Head Spa, Waxing). Below, rows: name in bold, duration and count line, price, and a Book button on the right. A "Save 13%" tag appears under some prices.
- Sources: Fresha (S6).
- Why a member values it: they can compare options by length and cost.

**D5. Studio profile with day chips.**
- Layout: cover photo, name, one-line description, tabs (Classes, Appointments, Pricing). Row of day chips with a date and slot count or "Unavailable". Class row below with time, room, capacity ("0/12"), and a Book button.
- Sources: Time2book (S3).
- Why a member values it: it shows real availability by day.

### Classes and programs

**C1. Class card with photo, instructor avatar, and Book pill.**
- Layout: wide card. Photo on the left half. Title, "w/ instructor name", date and time, small icons (share, repeat), and a rounded "Book →" button at the bottom right. Instructor photo as a round avatar at the top right of the card.
- Sources: Open (S2).
- Why a member values it: they see the instructor and the time together.

**C2. Program card with dates, weekly schedule, and instructor.**
- Layout: three-column grid. Each card has a bold title, a short description (3 lines), a dates line with a calendar icon, a schedule line ("3 sessions/week"), an instructor avatar with name and role, and a full-width "Learn More" button.
- Sources: Codecademy (S21).
- Why a member values it: a program's length, frequency, and teacher are clear before they click.

**C3. Level ladder.**
- Layout: vertical stack of cards. Each card has a level badge (A1, A2, B1), a title, a unit count, and one line of description. A state line shows "In progress" when relevant.
- Sources: Babbel (S21).
- Why a member values it: a clear path from beginner to advanced. Works for swim levels, strength programs, or kids club ages.

**C4. Class detail page with tag, host line, and sticky card.**
- Layout: photo banner. Tag above the title ("In-person activity"). Title. Host line ("by GrowFit | Redwood City"). Rating and review count. Address with pin. Tabs (Overview, Reviews, Location). Overview row with icons for Ages, Attendance, Frequency. Sticky booking card on the right.
- Sources: Care.com (S5).
- Why a member values it: they see age range, group size, and how often the class runs.

**C5. Event detail with date row, location link, and price block.**
- Layout: title. Icon row for date and time with a "repeats" note. Icon row for location with "View map". Price block with a Register button. About text with a "more" toggle. Cancellation line.
- Sources: Sweatpals (S4).
- Why a member values it: the basic facts are in a fixed order, and the price and button are easy to find.

**C6. Booking modal with confirmed state.**
- Layout: modal with image on the left. Confirmed state at top. Class name, teacher, date, time, location link. Manage button. Description. Cancellation policy.
- Sources: Open (S2).
- Why a member values it: they can review and change a booking without leaving the page.

### Schedule preview

**S-1. Day strip with the active day marked.**
- Layout: row of seven short day labels with dates. The active day has an underline (Open, S2) or a filled circle (Sweatpals, S4).
- Why a member values it: a quick way to pick a day.

**S-2. List schedule grouped by date.**
- Layout: date header. Rows with time and duration in a left column. Class title and type tag. Avatar stack of instructors or attendees. Price. Book button.
- Sources: Sweatpals (S4). Apple (S11) uses a simpler list with date header, title, time, location, and a Details button.
- Why a member values it: a dense day view that scans quickly.

**S-3. Weekly calendar grid.**
- Layout: seven columns. Each class is a card with time, name, instructor, duration, spots left, and Book.
- Sources: Wix template (S25). The copy is placeholder text. Use the structure only.
- Why a member values it: shows the whole week at once. Works best on desktop.

**S-4. Empty day state.**
- Layout: a grey or empty column with "No classes". Or a row of "Unavailable" labels.
- Sources: Wix (S25) and Time2book (S3).
- Why a member values it: they know the day has no classes. Avoid repeating "Unavailable" in every cell.

### Membership and pricing

**P1. Three tiers with a highlighted middle tier.**
- Layout: three cards side by side. The middle card is raised, has a border or dark fill, and carries a "Most popular" badge.
- Sources: OpenTable (S14) uses "Free to try", "Most popular", and "Most features" badges. Save.design (S15) and Deputy (S15) put a badge on the middle card.
- Why a member values it: one clear recommendation. Less decision work for a first-time member.

**P2. Monthly and yearly toggle with a savings tag.**
- Layout: toggle above the cards. Labels "Pay monthly" and "Pay yearly". A savings tag ("Save 30%") appears on the yearly option or as a corner ribbon on each card.
- Sources: Oku (S15) and Save.design (S15, "-17%").
- Why a member values it: they see a lower price per month for a longer commitment.

**P3. Tier card with price, period, one-line fit, features, and button.**

## 3. Specific design rules we should adopt

Each rule is testable. The "Source" line names the pattern it comes from.

1. Location card photo uses a 4:3 ratio and shows a real photo of that club. Source: L5, L2.
2. Location card shows club name, city, status line, distance, three amenity chips, and one primary button "Tour this club". Source: L2, L3.
3. Each location card has exactly one filled button. Source: N1, L2.
4. Status line uses words and color. Example: "Open · until 9pm" in saguaro green, "Closed · opens 5:30am" in copper. Color alone never carries the status. Source: L2.
5. Distance shows in miles with one decimal ("1.7 mi"). The distance hides when the visitor has not entered a ZIP or allowed location. Source: L2.
6. Desktop location page uses a split view: list left, map right, with the map at least 50% of the width. Source: L1.
7. Hovering a location card highlights its map pin, and clicking a pin scrolls the list to that card. Source: L1.
8. Map clusters show a count bubble. Single pins show the club short name. Source: L1.
9. Amenity filter chips appear above the location list. Use six or fewer chips. Chips for pool, courts, cycling, kids club, recovery, and group classes. Source: L3.
10. Location detail page has tabs in this order: Classes, Amenities, Hours and Map, Coaches, Reviews. Source: D1.
11. Location detail has a sticky booking card on desktop with one primary button "Tour this club" and one secondary button "Call the club". Source: D3.
12. On mobile, the booking card becomes a fixed bottom bar with the same two buttons. Source: D3, M3.
13. Hours table has seven rows. Today's row is bold. Holiday hours appear in a separate line under the table. Source: L7.
14. Class card shows title, instructor name with a 40 px round avatar, time, duration, intensity label, and a "Book" button. Source: C1.
15. Spots-left text appears only when five or fewer spots remain. Source: S-2 (Open, Time2book pattern).
16. Schedule preview shows a seven-day strip with today selected by default, and at most six classes before a "See full schedule" link. Source: S-1, S-2.
17. Class filter tabs use six or fewer categories. Source: C1, C2.
18. Pricing page shows three tiers. The middle tier has a "Most popular" badge and a raised border. Source: P1.
19. Pricing toggle defaults to monthly. Yearly shows the saving in words. Source: P2.
20. Each tier card lists six or fewer features. The full comparison goes below in an accordion or table. Source: P3, P4.
21. Each tier card shows a one-line "Best for" statement. Source: P3, P6.
22. Free tour is the main call to action on the home page, the location pages, and the class pages. The header button uses the same wording on all pages. Source: TC1.
23. Tour request form asks for four fields or fewer: name, email or phone, preferred club (prefilled), preferred time. No card fields. Source: TC3.
24. Tour request has a three-step flow: choose club and date, choose time, confirm. Each step shows a progress indicator. Source: TC2.
25. Tour confirmation shows the club address, parking note, and an "Add to calendar" link. Source: TC2, TC3.
26. Every instructor profile uses a 4:5 portrait photo, a name, one credential line, one "favorite class" line, and a bio of 25 words or fewer. Source: T1, T2, T6.
27. Every instructor profile includes the clubs where they teach. Source: T2.
28. Testimonials show a first name, a last initial, and a short line about the goal. Show at least three on the home page. Source: SP1, SP2.
29. Testimonial carousel shows one quote at a time, with arrows and dots, and quotes of 30 words or fewer. Source: SP2.
30. FAQ has eight to ten questions in three groups: Getting started, Memberships, and Kids and teens. The first question is open. Source: F1, F2.
31. FAQ includes at least one question about age rules for teens and older members. Source: F4.
32. FAQ ends with a line and link for "Still have a question?" that opens a contact form or call line. Source: F5.
33. Footer has four link columns: Clubs, Classes, Membership, About. Source: FT1.
34. Footer has a column or row with hours for all five clubs, using the same format as L7. Source: FT2.
35. Footer includes App Store and Google Play badges only if the app exists. Source: FT1.
36. Header shows five top-level items or fewer, plus one filled button "Tour a club". Source: N1.
37. Mega menu appears only for Clubs and Classes. Other items are plain links. Source: N2.
38. Mobile header uses a drawer. The drawer shows a "Find a club" search field at the top. Tap targets are at least 44 px high. Source: N4.
39. Hero headline is eight words or fewer. Support text is 20 words or fewer. The hero has one primary button and one secondary button. Source: H1.
40. Hero has no autoplay video with sound. A still photo or muted loop is allowed. Source: H5.
41. Trust row under the hero has three items with check icons, all short. Source: H2.
42. Amenities use three columns on desktop and one column on mobile. Each item has an icon in a circle, a two-line heading, and one line of text. Source: A1.
43. Body text meets a contrast ratio of 4.5 to 1 on sand and white backgrounds. Source: A3 (greyed items are a warning).
44. Use copper for primary buttons, saguaro green for open and available status, and sand for alternate section backgrounds. Source: brand brief.
45. Use real club photos only. Each club has its own photo in the location card and on its detail page. Source: L5, D2.
46. Each club page has its own address, phone, hours, parking note, and a kids club note if the club has one. Source: D2, L7.

## 4. Anti-patterns seen that we should avoid

- Neon gradient hero with saturated colors (Sweatpals, S4; Posh, S1-adjacent hero). Conflicts with the calm desert palette.
- Cookie banner placed on top of the hero copy (Time2book, S3). Place the banner at the bottom and keep it small.
- Required card for a free trial (Duolingo, S19; Motion, S19; Behance, S19). Our tour request must not ask for a card.
- Billing terms that look like the main text on the trial page (Duolingo, S19). Keep trial terms in small text under the button.
- Repeated "Unavailable" in every day cell (Time2book studio page, S3). Show a single empty state instead.
- Placeholder copy left in a template ("Service Name", "Staff", "Duration") (Wix, S25). Never ship placeholder text.
- Tiny day labels on a dark strip (Open, S2). Keep day labels at 14 px or larger.
- Light grey text on white for filter labels and disabled items (Urban Outfitters, S10). Check contrast before using grey.
- Icon-heavy store cards with six or more actions each (Urban Outfitters, S10). Limit each card to one primary action and one link.
- Long city link wall in the footer (ClassPass, S1). Limit the footer to five club links.
- Mega menu with 30 or more links (Higgsfield, S22; Hers, S22). Keep menus to four columns and about 20 links.
- Plan modal that blocks the page and forces a choice (Deputy, S15; QuickBooks, S15). Use a page, not a modal, for membership choice.
- Compare tables with 15 or more feature rows (Deputy, S15; Assembly, S15). Trim the table for a first-time visitor.
- Mixed typefaces inside one modal (QuickBooks upgrade modal, S15). Use one heading style across the site.
- Hero headline placed over busy photo detail (Posh, S1-adjacent hero). Keep the headline on a calm area of the image.
